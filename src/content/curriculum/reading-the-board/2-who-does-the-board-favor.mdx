You defend your big blind with eight-seven suited against a button raise. The flop comes ace-king-four. You have absolutely nothing. The button bets small — and here's the uncomfortable truth: this flop was never yours to fight for.

Not because of your two cards. Because of everyone's cards.

## Boards pick sides

Think back to preflop. The button raised, which means their range — all the hands they'd raise — is stuffed with aces, kings, and big pairs. You called from the big blind, so your range is full of suited connectors, medium cards, and small pairs. Two different armies.

Now deal ace-king-four. Whose army just got stronger? Theirs, massively. They hold every ace-king, ace-queen, and big pair. You mostly hold... eight-seven. When a board connects with one range far more than the other, that side has range advantage — and the side that has it gets to bet relentlessly, even with its weak hands.

That's why the button can bet this flop with their whole range and print money. It's also why fighting back with nothing here is burning chips: the math of whose range hit is against you, no matter how brave you feel.

## Boards that switch sides

Now deal seven-six-five with two of a suit. Who does this one favor? Suddenly it's you. The raiser's ace-king just became two overcards on a minefield, while your calling range is full of sevens, sixes, straights, and pairs-plus-draws.

There's a name worth knowing here: capped. A range is capped when it can't contain the very strongest hands. The button who raised preflop almost never has seven-six or five-five here — they'd usually have played bigger pairs the same way, but the little connected hands that smash this board mostly aren't in their range at all. Your range isn't capped on this board. Theirs is. That asymmetry is power, and it's why good players start firing at boards like this from the big blind.

## The question to ask on every flop

Not "did I hit?" but "whose range hit?" High boards with big cards favor the preflop raiser. Low, connected boards favor the caller. When the board favors you, bet more often than feels natural. When it favors them, get out of the way with your air — folding a hopeless hand to a small bet isn't weak, it's accounting.

<KeyIdea>
  Every flop favors somebody's range. Figure out whose before you act — attack the boards that are
  yours, and stop paying rent on the ones that aren't.
</KeyIdea>
