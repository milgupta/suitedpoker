You already know the shape from the earlier lesson: fold the dominated hands, call the ones that keep their value, and 4-bet monsters plus a few carefully chosen bluffs. This module assumes that shape is settled. What changes now is how the shape stretches and shrinks when the seats change — because a button open facing a big-blind 3-bet is not the same decision as an under-the-gun open facing a middle-position 3-bet, even when both screens print the same three action buttons.

## Wider opens continue wider

The button opens nearly half the deck. When the big blind 3-bets, a large slice of that opening range still continues — more suited connectors, more broadway calls, and a thicker band of 4-bet bluffs built from suited wheel aces. The cutoff opened tighter than the button, so its continue drops the speculative end first. Middle position opened tighter still, which means the absolute continue is narrower even though, as a fraction of its own open, it looks denser.

That is why drilling “facing a 3-bet” without naming the seats teaches the wrong lesson. Position is not a footnote you add after you pick fold, call, or raise. Position is the range you are holding, and the range is the entire question.

## Blind 3-bets versus cold 3-bets

A big-blind 3-bet comes from a range that can be somewhat wide: they close the action and they get a discount relative to everyone else. A cold 3-bet from the button against your cutoff open is stronger on average. They had the option to flat your raise and chose instead to put in a re-raise with players still left to act behind them. Against that cold 3-bet you continue tighter, and you 4-bet-bluff less often, because their value-heavy core is denser.

Out of position the tax is larger still. Hands that are comfortable calls when you will act last on every street become folds when you will be guessing first in a pot three times the usual size. Let that tax break close decisions rather than ego.
