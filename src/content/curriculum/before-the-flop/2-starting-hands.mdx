Everyone folds to you. You look down at ace-seven offsuit. It has an ace in it. Aces are good. You raise.

Six months from now, this hand will still be costing you money — because ace-seven offsuit looks strong and plays weak. Learning which hands those are is the fastest upgrade in poker.

<HandExample hole="Ad 7c" label="Ace-seven offsuit: the classic looks-good, plays-bad hand." />

## What makes a hand worth opening

When you're the first raiser — called opening, or raising first in — you want hands that do well in the pots they create. Three things matter.

High cards that connect. Ace-king makes the best pair most of the time it pairs. Ace-seven makes the same pair of aces, but with a kicker — the side card that breaks ties — that loses to almost every other ace.

Suitedness. Two cards of the same suit can make a flush, and flushes win big pots. It's worth more than people think: king-nine suited is a fine button open. King-nine offsuit is borderline.

Connection. Cards close in rank — like nine-eight — can make straights. They rarely win unimproved, but when they hit, they hit disguised and get paid.

## The ranges, seat by seat

A range is simply the set of hands you'd play the same way. You met two ranges in the last lesson. Here's the middle of the spectrum:

<RangeGridEmbed node="CO:rfi" caption="Opening from the cutoff — one seat before the button." />

The pattern across all six seats is one idea, repeated: every seat closer to the button adds a layer of hands. Under the gun opens roughly the top 15%. The middle seats add more suited hands and medium pairs. The cutoff adds most suited aces and hands like king-ten. The button adds everything from ace-deuce offsuit to seven-five suited.

Why does the small blind open tight again, when only one player is left? Because that player — the big blind — will be in position on you for the whole hand. You act first on every street after the flop. That tax is real, and the range shrinks to pay for it.

## The habit that makes this stick

Don't memorize a hundred and sixty-nine hands. Anchor each seat to its border cases instead. Under the gun, ace-jack suited is a raise and ace-ten suited is close. On the button, king-deuce suited plays and queen-five offsuit doesn't. Learn the edges and everything inside them takes care of itself.

Then drill it. The chart fades in a week; a hundred reps of real decisions don't. That's what the practice button below this lesson is for.
