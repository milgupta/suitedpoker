Nobody goes broke folding seven-deuce. The hands that empty a beginner's bankroll are the pretty ones — the hands that look too good to fold and play just well enough to reach the expensive streets in second place.

Here's the rogues' gallery. Learn their faces.

## Ace-rag offsuit: the kicker trap

Ace-little offsuit — ace-eight and below, unsuited — is the most expensive hand class in beginner poker. The trap is mechanical: when the ace flops, you have top pair, and top pair feels unfoldable. But anyone else playing an ace has a better kicker, and the money goes in exactly when you're second best. You don't lose one big pot with ace-six. You lose forty medium ones, and they never feel like mistakes.

Against a raise, these hands are pure folds. Unopened in late position, they're raises — the problem was never the hand alone; it's the hand against a range that plays aces better.

## King-jack and queen-ten: the broadway impostors

Two picture cards read as premium to every new player. But walk their pairs through a raised pot: flop the jack with king-jack and you lose to ace-jack; flop the king and you lose to ace-king. Dominated hands from the 3-bet lesson, in their natural habitat. Playable when you're the raiser — trouble when you're the caller.

## Small pairs, played too far

Pocket fours are a fine hand with a simple mission: flop a set — that's three of a kind — or get out. The set arrives about one time in eight. The leak is what happens the other seven: calling the flop with an underpair "to see one more," calling the turn because the river might be a four. Miss the flop, lose the minimum. That's the whole skill.

## Suited changes the verdict

One qualifier before the checkpoint, because it decides a lot of close calls: the suited version of a trouble hand is a genuinely different hand. Ace-six suited can make the nut flush — the best possible one — and that single extra way to win turns a fold into a playable holding in the right spot. King-jack suited has the same upgrade.

It isn't a licence to play everything suited. It's a tiebreaker. When a hand is borderline, suited leans toward playing and offsuit leans toward folding, and the offsuit versions are the ones on this list for a reason.

## The pattern under all of them

Notice what these hands share: they make second-best pairs that feel like winners. The cost isn't the preflop chip — it's the three streets of calls afterward, each one feeling reasonable. So the fix lives preflop, where it's cheap. Fold the trouble hands against raises, and the expensive rivers simply never happen. Your win rate improves by mistakes subtracted, not heroes made.
