Ask a beginner why they just bet and you'll usually get a shrug: "I had a good hand." Ask them why they bet with nothing and it's "I wanted to take it down." Neither is a plan. Both lose money in the long run.

Every good bet has a job. There are only two real ones — and before chips go in, you should know which one this bet is doing.

## Job one: get paid by worse hands

This is a value bet — betting because a worse hand can call you. The test is brutally simple: name the worse hands that call. Out loud, if you have to.

Bet this and worse kings call. Nines call. Stubborn ace-highs sometimes call. That's a customer list, so the bet makes money. Now imagine betting a hand so strong nothing worse ever calls — or so weak nothing better ever folds. The chips go in, but the job doesn't exist. That's the shrug-bet, and naming the customer is the cure.

## Job two: make better hands fold

This is a bluff — betting so a hand that beats you gives up. And the same discipline applies: name the better hands that fold. A good bluff attacks hands with showdown value that can't stand pressure. Ace-high folds to your bet on a scary board? Real job. A flopped set folds? Never. Bluffing a calling station — a player who never folds — is setting money on fire with extra steps.

The best bluffs also bring backup: a draw that can still become the best hand if called. That's called a semi-bluff, and it's the safest aggression in poker — you win when they fold now, and you win when the flush arrives later.

## The half-job: protection

Sometimes you bet a medium hand — say, second pair on a wet board — not expecting worse to call much, but to charge the overcards and draws that would happily see a free turn. That's protection. It's a real reason on wet boards where a free card flips the hand, and a fake one on dry boards where nothing is coming. If the board is king-seven-two rainbow, "protection" is usually a shrug-bet wearing a vest.

## The habit

Before every bet, one sentence: "worse calls" or "better folds." If you can't finish the sentence, check. You'll be amazed how many of your old bets fail this test — and how much calmer the game gets when every chip has a job.

<KeyIdea>
  Never bet on a shrug: every bet either gets called by worse or folds out better. If you can't say
  which, check.
</KeyIdea>
